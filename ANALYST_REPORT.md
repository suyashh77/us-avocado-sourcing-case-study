# Global Avocado Supply Chain — Analyst Briefing

**As of September 26, 2026.** Fresh avocado imports are the focus. The most recent complete U.S. import year in this snapshot is 2025; the cross-source disruption calculation uses 2024, the aligned year for USDA ERS imports and FAOSTAT bilateral exports. Volumes are metric tonnes.

## Global landscape

FAOSTAT reports **11.23 million tonnes** of world avocado production in 2024, about **104% above 2015**. Mexico produced **2.76 million tonnes**, or **24.6%** of the 2024 world total. The Atlas has positive 2024 production observations for **73 producer countries**. These figures cover avocados generally, not only fresh Hass fruit qualified for U.S. retail. [FAOSTAT production](https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/QCL).

The largest 2024 exporter-reported bilateral record in the Atlas is **Mexico → United States, 1.03 million tonnes**. Peru → Netherlands (184,442 tonnes) and Peru → Spain (123,627 tonnes) follow. Summing reported export rows by destination gives **1.17 million tonnes to the United States** and **427,841 tonnes to the Netherlands**. These are reported trading partners, not mapped vessel or truck routes, and the Netherlands also reports substantial re-exports. Destination totals are therefore not final consumption. The U.S. stress test uses USDA ERS import records rather than these FAOSTAT exporter reports for its baseline. [FAOSTAT detailed trade matrix](https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/TM); [USDA ERS trade data](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/fruit-and-tree-nuts-data/trade-and-prices-by-category-and-commodity).

## Executive assessment

U.S. fresh-avocado imports reached **1,300,436 tonnes in 2025**. Mexico supplied **1,084,244 tonnes (83.4%)**, down from 87.6% of imports in 2024 but still the dominant origin. USDA ERS estimates imports accounted for 88.4% of domestic fresh-avocado availability in 2024. These ratios have different denominators and should not be multiplied into a single operational exposure measure. [USDA ERS trade data](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/fruit-and-tree-nuts-data/trade-and-prices-by-category-and-commodity); [USDA ERS Yearbook H-1](https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/fruit-and-tree-nuts-data/fruit-and-tree-nuts-yearbook-tables).

The 2024 stress test removes **1,066,358 tonnes** of Mexico-origin U.S. imports. Five alternative origins already selling to the U.S. reported **750,409 tonnes** of exports to non-U.S. destinations. Even redirecting every reported tonne leaves **315,949 tonnes** uncovered. This is a conditional ceiling for the existing U.S. supplier set, not free capacity or a global supply bound. Existing customers, product mix, access conditions, and timing make 100% diversion implausible. [FAOSTAT trade matrix](https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/TM); [USDA ERS trade data](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/fruit-and-tree-nuts-data/trade-and-prices-by-category-and-commodity).

## Retail sourcing decision and price evidence

The annual Mexico-zero case is an extreme bound. For day-to-day buying, the [short-horizon tabletop](CASE_STUDY.md#short-horizon-scenario-model) uses observed February and July 2025 monthly imports as weekly reference rates and varies the disruption length, Mexico availability, extra flow from other origins, and one-time usable buffer. Its preset residual gaps are **10,669 tonnes** for a one-week February pause, **25,556 tonnes** for an eight-week February squeeze, and **22,042 tonnes** for a two-week July interruption. These are modeled national import-equivalent gaps, not forecasts of retail shortage or retail price.

The price story is deliberately separated by measure. [USDA ERS's March 2022 outlook](https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/outlooks/105859/FTS-374.pdf) documents a smaller Mexican crop, 17% lower January U.S. imports, and January shipping-point carton prices of **$48.94 versus $23.05** a year earlier; the February 12–18 inspection pause occurred after prices were already elevated. [HAB/Circana's retail composite](https://hassavocadoboard.com/wp-content/uploads/Total-U.S.-2024-Q4.pdf) records annual U.S. category average selling prices of **$1.28, $1.06, and $1.19 per fruit** in 2022, 2023, and 2024. [USDA ERS's March 2026 outlook](https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/outlooks/113982/FTS-384.pdf) links improved large-fruit availability to lower FOB prices in 2025/26. These sources cannot isolate a causal retail-price response to any single event.

## Alternative sourcing lanes

|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Peru | 100,739 | 503,202 | June–August, 69.7% of annual U.S. flow | Largest reported alternative export pool, strong summer bridge, competing export customers. |

| Chile | 9,772 | 100,988 | January–March, 53.5% | Export presence exceeds the observed U.S. channel; route and commercial qualification needed. |

The U.S. import volumes are USDA ERS country-of-origin observations; the export volumes are FAOSTAT exporter-reported flows to other partners. They cannot be added as independent supply. The peak windows are descriptive, not capacity estimates. USDA's [March 2026 outlook](https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/outlooks/113982/FTS-384.pdf) also finds that Peru's U.S. shipments cluster in summer, Colombia's are less seasonal, and more than half of Peru's and Colombia's fresh exports go to the EU. The Dominican Republic supplies nearly all U.S. non-Hass-like imports in the report.

California is a **domestic seasonal complement**, with shipments building through spring and peaking in summer according to the same USDA outlook. It is outside the import-reallocation model. Guatemala is a **development option**: [APHIS's April 2025 update](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/news/program-update/update-import-requirements-guatemala-fresh-avocado-fruit) specifies a systems approach and pest-control workplan for Hass exports. The ERS snapshot records no Guatemala-to-U.S. flow in 2024 or 2025, so it is not counted as existing replacement capacity.

## Risk register

1. **Concentration and entry:** Mexico supplied 83.4% of 2025 import volume, while 86% of 2024 imports entered through the Laredo customs district. These are distinct measures; together they justify continuity planning around the dominant origin and corridor. [USDA ERS trade data](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/fruit-and-tree-nuts-data/trade-and-prices-by-category-and-commodity); [March 2025 outlook](https://esmis.nal.usda.gov/sites/default/release-files/h989r3203/2n49vz489/k643cx565/FTS-381.pdf).
2. **Calendar fit:** Mexico supplied 95.2% of January–April 2024 imports. Peru sent only 1,769 tonnes to the U.S. in those months, versus 43,187 in June–July. Annual exports cannot cover an out-of-season weekly shortfall by assumption. [USDA ERS trade data](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/fruit-and-tree-nuts-data/trade-and-prices-by-category-and-commodity).
3. **Competing buyers:** Peru and Colombia's export volumes to Europe and other markets are already in trade. Reallocation requires a commercial agreement and may displace current customers. [FAOSTAT trade matrix](https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/TM); [March 2026 outlook](https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/outlooks/113982/FTS-384.pdf).
4. **Product and market access:** Hass and non-Hass fruit are not interchangeable for every buyer. USDA [APHIS requirements](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/how-to-import) vary by commodity and origin and must be checked in ACIR before shipments are planned.

## Retail-ready supply is a separate constraint

The [Hass Avocado Board's Circana study](https://hassavocadoboard.com/wp-content/uploads/hab-research-insights-rethinking-retail-avocado-bagged.pdf) puts bagged avocados at **19.0% of U.S. retail fruit units in 2019** and **28.4% in 2022**, a 9.4-point increase. The denominator counts individual avocados, including fruit inside bags. In 2022, small 4- and 5-count bags accounted for **54% of bagged avocado dollar sales**. This is a historical format signal, not a current-year share or a measure of export capacity. The 2015–2020 dataset in the separate Avocado research folder had bag columns with an unclear unit definition; its much higher bag-share calculation was not imported.

The four annual chart observations are transcribed in [retail_format_2019_2022.csv](data/processed/retail_format_2019_2022.csv) from the HAB report's page 11 retail-unit chart; the small-bag dollar share comes from page 21. Chart shares are the publisher's rounded figures, so recomputing from the one-decimal million-unit values may differ by 0.1 percentage point.

For a retail replacement program, require weekly packhouse capacity by fruit size and bag count, packaging inputs, ripening and quality specifications, and delivered cost by SKU. A tonne of exportable fruit is not necessarily a tonne in the format a retailer has contracted to sell.

## Decisions to validate

- **Now:** Confirm Mexico lane continuity, alternate customs and inspection arrangements, and inventory assumptions with named suppliers and importers.
- **Next procurement cycle:** Test Peru for summer coverage and Colombia for broader first-half coverage. Obtain firm weekly volume, grade, ripening, transit, and landed-cost data.
- **Strategic:** Pilot smaller channels such as Chile; assess California as a domestic seasonal buffer and Guatemala as a qualification project. Count volume only after market access, contracts, and logistics are verified.

The remaining inputs for an executable sourcing plan are uncommitted weekly supply, release terms with current buyers, packhouse and cold-chain throughput, current APHIS eligibility, transit reliability, and demand response. The Atlas's cost-premium slider is illustrative arithmetic; it cannot predict retail prices.
