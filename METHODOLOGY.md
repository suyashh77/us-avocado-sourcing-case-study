# Methodology and audit

## Question and grain

The unit of analysis is a country-year production or bilateral export record, and a U.S.-origin-month import record. The annual extreme scenario comparison year is 2024, the latest common complete year established for FAOSTAT trade and the U.S. import series. The short-horizon scenario uses observed 2025 U.S. monthly imports. The U.S. import trend extends through 2025. All weights are metric tonnes.

## Ingestion and validation

1. Download the original FAOSTAT normalized ZIP archives and USDA ERS CSV. Raw files are never edited.
2. Filter FAOSTAT to item 572 and years 2015 onward. Preserve original labels, codes, units and flags in staging CSV files.
3. Use FAOSTAT production element 5510 in tonnes. Exclude blank values and regional/group aggregates from country rows. The 2024 source has 18 blank production observations across the extracted 2015–2024 interval; no values are imputed. The published FAOSTAT World observation is retained separately.
4. Use FAOSTAT reporter export-quantity observations in tonnes for 2024 bilateral flows. Aggregate a supplier's reported non-U.S. partner exports; do not add mirrored partner import reports to exports.
5. Use USDA ERS `Trade=Import`, `MarketSegment=Fresh`, `CommodityName=Avocados`, both Hass-like and Unspecified details. Sum the detail rows by country, calendar year and month, 2017–2025. Convert thousand pounds to tonnes and thousand dollars to dollars.
6. Reconcile each monthly ERS World volume with the sum of named origins. All 108 months (2017–2025) agree within 0.05 tonne after source rounding. Country-year and bilateral 2024 keys have no duplicate rows in processed output.
7. Read USDA ERS Yearbook Table H-1's calendar-2024 fresh avocado import share of domestic availability directly (88.42349%). This is a USDA balance-sheet estimate with a different denominator from the Atlas's origin shares.

The normalized snapshot contains 851 country-year production rows, 1,077 bilateral 2024 export edges, and 565 U.S. origin-month import rows. Country production rows sum to 11,221,448 tonnes in 2024, 4,638 tonnes (0.041%) below the published 11,226,086-tonne World figure; no artificial balancing row is created.

## U.S. baseline and cross-source discrepancy

ERS World U.S. fresh-avocado imports: 1,217,195 tonnes in 2024. ERS Mexico origin: 1,066,358 tonnes, or 87.61%. FAOSTAT records 1,025,674 tonnes as Mexico-reported exports to the United States; the U.S.-reported/ERS import view is 40,684 tonnes higher (3.8% of the ERS Mexico figure). The Atlas uses ERS imports for the shock and exposes the reporting difference. These series should not be added together.

As a separate code-level check, UN Comtrade's U.S.-reported 2024 HS 080440 import net weight is 1,217,218 tonnes worldwide and 1,066,359 tonnes from Mexico, just 23 and 1 tonnes above the ERS figures respectively. HS 080440 includes **fresh or dried** avocados, so this is a consistency check rather than a substitute for the ERS fresh series. USDA ERS FATUS lists 2024 avocado import value as $3,767,841,541, exactly equal to the ERS fresh-avocado annual value in this snapshot. FATUS and ERS are USDA views of U.S. customs data, not independent physical measurements. `pipeline/validate_comtrade.py` and `pipeline/validate_fatus.py` reproduce these checks; Comtrade's preview endpoint is rate-limited.

## Scenario calculation

### Short-horizon retail sourcing tabletop

The buyer-facing tabletop uses USDA ERS origin-month import observations for February and July 2025. Each month is converted to an average weekly rate by multiplying tonnes by `7 / calendar days in month`. The monthly average is repeated for the selected number of weeks; it is a planning baseline, not an observed weekly series. February's baseline is 93,900.7 tonnes total, including 77,542.7 from Mexico; July's is 108,089.69 total, including 71,446.29 from Mexico.

`lost Mexico tonnes = baseline Mexico weekly tonnes × shock weeks × (1 − Mexico availability fraction)`

`extra alternative tonnes = baseline non-Mexico weekly tonnes × shock weeks × alternative-flow uplift fraction`

`usable buffer tonnes = min(lost Mexico tonnes, baseline Mexico weekly tonnes × buffer days / 7)`

`uncovered import-equivalent gap = max(0, lost Mexico tonnes − extra alternative tonnes − usable buffer tonnes)`

The buffer is credited once, not once per week. Each extra alternative tonne is an assumption about increased flow relative to its observed baseline, not verified free capacity. Alternative origins may differ in variety, size, grade, season, transit time, food safety requirements, and consumer acceptance. Results are national import-equivalent planning arithmetic; they are neither a retailer-specific stockout forecast nor an estimate of retail price response. Preset assumptions and outputs are stored with the source observations in `dist/case_data.json` and generated by `pipeline/short_horizon_model.py`.

### Annual extreme supplier diversion

For each eligible supplier `i`:

`redirect_i = (FAOSTAT exports from i to all non-U.S. partners in 2024) × selected diversion fraction`

`uncovered gap = max(0, ERS U.S. imports from Mexico − sum(redirect_i))`

The model intentionally does not call exports to others an exportable surplus. They are already traded volumes and may be contractually or physically unavailable. The default 20% diversion leaves roughly 916,000 tonnes uncovered; 50% leaves roughly 691,000 tonnes. These are conditional arithmetic results.

At the deliberately extreme 100% diversion setting, the five origins with observed 2024 U.S. imports and FAOSTAT non-U.S. exports have 750,408.59 tonnes of reported exports to other destinations, leaving 315,949.03 tonnes of Mexican U.S. import volume uncovered. This is a conditional ceiling **within the current U.S. supplier set**, not a worldwide production or export capacity limit. It would require stripping all those volumes from their existing buyers, while assuming every tonne meets U.S. requirements and can arrive in time. The scenario does not include new countries gaining U.S. access or extra production.

## Concentration and seasonal exposure

The main-page Sankey and adjacent comparison use **only FAOSTAT exporter-reported 2024 bilateral trade** for Peru, Colombia, and Chile. Each supplier's reported destination is grouped into United States, Netherlands, Spain, United Kingdom, or Other buyers. The latter sums every remaining reported destination; flows are proportional to reported tonnes. The U.S. portion in these figures is FAOSTAT reporter-side exports, not the USDA ERS U.S.-reported import series used in the shock scenarios. No producer output or mirror import record is added to these ribbons. Partner relationships may involve re-exports and are not physical routes or uncommitted capacity.

For each 2017–2025 calendar year, the Mexico share equals ERS Mexico-origin imports divided by ERS World imports. The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) is the sum of squared origin shares multiplied by 10,000; the inverse of the unscaled sum is the effective number of equally sized origins. These are descriptive concentration statistics, not a probability of disruption. In 2025, Mexico's recorded share was 83.4%, down from 87.6% in 2024, while annual U.S. fresh avocado imports reached about 1.300 million tonnes. The interactive scenario remains anchored to 2024 cross-source data.

The monthly exposure chart divides ERS Mexico-origin volume by ERS World volume for each month of 2024. January–April combined was 95.2% Mexico, compared with 59.9% in June–July; Peru's recorded U.S. shipments were 1,769 and 43,187 tonnes in those periods. These observations reveal when alternate lanes were active. They do not establish monthly spare capacity or a feasible diversion schedule.

The alternative-origin profiles compare four material or analytically distinctive observed lanes. U.S. import totals use USDA ERS calendar 2024 and 2025 observations. The peak three-month window is the largest sum of three consecutive calendar months in each origin's 2025 U.S. shipments, divided by that origin's annual U.S. total. Reported non-U.S. exports and leading destinations use FAOSTAT exporter-side 2024 records. A peak window describes the U.S. channel's historical timing and does not establish harvest capacity. Trade destinations may be distribution hubs or re-export points. The Dominican Republic's non-Hass distinction, California's seasonal role, and Peru/Colombia EU competition are sourced to USDA ERS's March 2026 outlook. Guatemala's regulatory pathway is sourced to USDA APHIS, with no current volume credited in the scenario.

## Historical calibration limit

ERS annual fresh-avocado imports fell from about 1.262 million tonnes in 2023 to 1.217 million in 2024, while nominal customs import value rose from about $2.98 billion to $3.77 billion. This coincident movement cannot identify a supply elasticity or the effect of losing Mexico entirely: quality, origin mix, exchange rates, other demand and supply conditions also changed. The premium control is therefore labeled an assumption and the output a cost illustration. The model has **not** been validated as a price forecast.

## Retail price observations

The Hass Avocado Board/Circana Total U.S. retail composite reports annual category average selling price per unit of $1.28 in 2022, $1.06 in 2023, and $1.19 in 2024. This is observed retail sales dollars divided by retail units, a different measure from USDA AMS advertised prices and USDA ERS price per pound. It is presented as a transaction-based annual series, not as an event study or a model calibration target.

The March 2022 USDA ERS outlook reports a January 2022 shipping-point price of $48.94 for two-layer Hass cartons of sizes 40, 48, and 60, versus $23.05 one year earlier. That rise preceded the February 12–18 inspection suspension; the report also describes a smaller Mexican crop. The June 2024 inspection pause and the reported 2024 shift toward smaller fruit are dated context, not identified causes of the annual retail ASP change. USDA ERS's March 2026 outlook describes a recovery in large-fruit availability and a mid-March FOB price near $1/lb, about one-third of its year-earlier comparison. Shipping-point cartons, FOB per pound, annual retail ASP per unit, and advertised retail prices must not be joined into one continuous price series or interpreted as a single pass-through relationship.

The 2023 USDA ERS Fruit and Vegetable Prices workbook gives a national fresh-avocado average retail price of $2.2624 per pound, estimated from Circana scanner data. ERS warns that differences in underlying product coding and methods make its sparse annual releases unsuitable for direct year-to-year comparisons. The Atlas uses one 2023 benchmark only.

The USDA AMS September 25, 2026 Weekly Grocery Store Specialty Crops report gives a national weighted average **advertised** price for conventional Hass avocados sold individually: $0.88 each across 7,225 ads. The same report lists $0.94 in the prior week and $1.05 in the comparable week a year earlier for the same reported item. The survey covers major retailers' circular and digital promotions, not all transactions or a representative basket of fruit weights. The report is a rolling latest PDF; its report date is stored with the processed observation. No per-fruit-to-per-pound conversion is attempted. Neither measure is used to predict retail effects of the supply shock.

## Interpretation limits

FAOSTAT item 572 and USDA's fresh-avocado market segment are close but not identical definitions. Annual FAOSTAT bilateral trade does not establish the physical route, port, transit time, season, varietal mix, free capacity, or contract position. The customs district shares in the USDA outlook are contextual observed facts, not capacity limits. Production is not exportable supply. A complete operational substitution model needs monthly exporter supply, grades, market-access rules, logistics capacity, competing buyer prices, and a defensible demand response.
