# Editorial critique: U.S. avocado sourcing case study

**Review lens:** a sourcing analyst at a U.S. retailer that sells fresh avocados. Reviewed September 26, 2026.

## What the previous Atlas did well

- It grounded origin concentration in USDA ERS import volume and kept 2024 and 2025 denominators visible.
- It distinguished FAOSTAT production and bilateral exports from immediately available, U.S.-qualified fruit.
- Its Mexico-zero calculation made the size of the tail risk legible, and its source ledger made most claims auditable.

## What a retail buying team would challenge

| Issue | Why it weakens a sourcing decision | Revision |
|---|---|---|
| The dramatic Mexico-zero year dominated the story. | Buyers first plan for days and weeks of disruption, then for a prolonged crop shortage. An annual total hides inventory and seasonal timing. | Added one-week, two-week, and eight-week tableaus using observed 2025 monthly import baselines. Kept the annual test as an extreme bound. |
| Production and trade volumes looked too close to supplier capacity. | Fruit may be committed, wrong-sized, non-Hass, inaccessible under APHIS rules, or impossible to pack and ripen for the retailer. | Added explicit alternative-flow and usable-buffer assumptions, SKU/size qualification, and a list of supplier evidence needed before contracting. |
| Price points used different units and market stages. | A promotional price per fruit, average retail price per pound, shipping-point carton price, and customs import unit value cannot form one price series. | Added a consistent HAB/Circana annual average-selling-price series per fruit; separated FOB and shipping-point event evidence. |
| News appeared as a risk anecdote. | A portfolio case study should date each disruption and show the observed price and supply context without treating correlation as causation. | Added 2022, 2024, and 2025/26 episodes with official USDA and HAB sources and explicit attribution limits. |
| The scenario had no service metric. | Buyers need to know the unfilled volume and its share of the reference demand period. | Added a residual-tonne bridge and unfilled import-equivalent percentage. |
| The recommendation was broad. | “Diversify” does not specify windows, contract terms, or decision triggers. | Reframed the decision around Mexico continuity, season-specific Peru/Colombia trials, pack-format qualification, usable buffer, and weekly data/contract requirements. |

## Residual limitations

The new short-horizon model still uses **national imports as a proxy**, not the retailer's POS forecast, DC inventory, contracted fill, waste, store demand, or margin. Its extra-origin flow and buffer values are assumptions. The price episodes are descriptive and cannot estimate the causal effect of a suspension, weather, or assortment shift. The next professional step would join retailer SKU-level sales, purchase orders, landed costs, ripening loss, and supplier weekly commitments with USDA market data.
